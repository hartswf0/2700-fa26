House of Silt

In 1967 Alison Knowles wrote four lists and a FORTRAN program picked one word from each, printing quatrain after quatrain: The House of Dust. In 1970 she built one of them at CalArts. Trouttown was built the same way. Every house below is one pick from four arrays, and each word selects the routine that draws it.

How the town is built

The zoning office prints a quatrain. The paper drifts downstream to an empty lot. The house goes up in four steps, one per line: the material, the place, the light, who lives there.

The frame (fixed)
A HOUSE OF [material]
  [location]
    [light]
      INHABITED BY [inhabitants]
The lists (the paradigm)

The frame is the syntax and never changes. Each list is a set of words that can fill one slot. A house is one word from each list, bound into one object.

    Syntax and paradigm
    The frame A HOUSE OF [ ] / [ ] / [ ] / INHABITED BY [ ] is the fixed sequence. Each array is a column of words that can stand in one slot. random(materials) is a pick from one column.
    
    
    Words as switches
    The program never reads English. h.material === "TIN" selects the corrugation routine. A word with no routine, like LEGO after you push it, can be printed but only drawn as a gray block with its name on it.
    
    
    The edge of the town
    Nothing outside the four arrays can be built. The count is the product of their lengths. Push a word and the town can hold more houses; delete one and some houses become impossible.
