DESCRIPTION
The district put attendance on the old TV in Room 4. It marks Finley Current with a C every morning, even the one morning he is early. Ms. Rainey turns the TV around and the class reads the program: a bubble that won't move until someone calls it, a Lost & Found drawer that starts at slot zero, and a crosswalk on Main Street painted by a loop. The bug in the attendance loop is one number.

This is the Review cut, written for professional viewers. The same episode exists in three cuts: Middle School, College, and Professional. The story is the same; the code on the board and the explanations change.

WHAT THIS CUT COVERS
- Inversion of control: you register setup() and draw(); the runtime owns the loop.
- A pure step(state, bounds, r) with clamping, plus invariants checked every frame (inside the box; |v| conserved).
- Frame-rate dependence: the same step() on a 120 Hz display runs twice as fast. Measure speed per second with deltaTime.
- A cyclic cursor as a small immutable module with an explicit invariant; recorded decisions for the empty case and for removal.
- Stable IDs over indexes: slots move, claim numbers don't.
- A hardcoded loop bound as a latent defect.
- Layout as data from pure functions; hidden global drawing state (rectMode) and push()/pop() to contain it.
- The TV screens in this cut are drawn by the lesson code's own functions: step(), Carousel, layout().

THE SKETCHES
Nine working p5.js sketches go with the episode: three lessons (functions, arrays, loops) at three levels. Paste any of them into editor.p5js.org and press Play. Each ends with things to try. The professional sketches export their pure functions and come with a test file you can run with node.

HOW IT WAS MADE
Every frame is drawn by code on a canvas, with a multiplane camera: each drawing sits on a plane at a set depth, and near planes move faster than far ones. The school's mill wheel turns a frame counter; the counter in the credits stops at the number of frames in this cut. The episode is one draw function called 6,262 times.
