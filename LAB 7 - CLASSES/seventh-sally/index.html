03 · THE SEVENTH SALLY

The Seventh Sally

How Trurl’s Own Perfection Led to No Good

NARRATION

Excelsius the tyrant had been thrown off his throne by his own subjects. Now he sat on a bare asteroid, still demanding someone to rule.

Trurl would not give him real people to rule. So he built him a kingdom in a box: towers, roads, seasons, and thousands of small citizens, each one able to think.

The box was small enough to carry. Inside it was a whole country.

The king ruled it from a console. A lever for taxes, a lever for the census, a lever for pardons. A flick of the finger, and a decree went out.

Inside, every citizen walked its own street, and loved, and was afraid.

Time ran faster in the box. One afternoon of royal games was a century of their history.

Trurl. It is only a model. Their groans are numbers.

Klapaucius. If the model is perfect, the suffering is perfect too. You did not build a toy. You built subjects.

Trurl. Then I will free them. I will hold an election.

Klapaucius. You made them lords and vassals. To free them, you would have to remake them.

Trurl. And where does remaking end, and tampering with minds begin?

They came too late. The citizens had broken the glass, taken the asteroid, and built ships of their own.

And above their small world hung a new, pale moon, with the stern face of Excelsius.

FOR THE BENCH

The box is a class with a constructor and an update(). Trurl wrote both. Excelsius only ever touched the parameters: taxes, laws, pardons.

The citizens kept running their own update(), faster than the king could watch, until it took them out of the box.

Watch the scale box in the corner of each shot. It is the only number in the film.
