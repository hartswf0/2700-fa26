Getting the films onto YouTube

Twenty-two films, an opening-titles video, and the complete two-hour cut. For every video there is a title, a description, tags, a thumbnail and an English caption file. Copy each field into YouTube Studio as you upload.

Links that go into the descriptions

The descriptions point to the playlist and to the complete cut. Make those two first (step 2 below), paste their links here, and every description on this page fills them in. This browser remembers what you paste.

Order of work

  1. Verify the channel in YouTube Studio (Settings, Channel, Feature eligibility; a phone number is enough). Without it YouTube refuses custom thumbnails and anything longer than 15 minutes.
  2. Make the playlist first, and start the complete cut. Create an empty playlist with the title and description below and copy its link. Start uploading the complete cut; the upload window shows the video's link straight away. Paste both links above.
  3. Upload the films as Private, up to 15 at a time. For each one: paste the title, description and tags; upload the thumbnail; under Subtitles, add English with "Upload file", "With timing", and the .srt. Set the settings in the table below.
  4. Fill the playlist in order: the opening titles, then films 01 to 22.
  5. Upload the opening titles and set them as the channel trailer (Customisation, Layout).
  6. Add end screens to each film in the last 20 seconds: the next film and the playlist. The films end on a quiet card, which leaves room for them.
  7. Watch one film all the way through on YouTube with captions on, then schedule the rest to go Public.

Upload filesThe original renders, 1280 × 720, 24 fps, H.264 and AAC. Each film's "Video file" button downloads its master. The complete cut is too large to keep here; it is the files joined end to end, sent in the conversation as two parts.

A note on rights

Lem's stories and their English translations are under copyright. The films paraphrase rather than quote and credit every source, but they are still adaptations, and a rights holder can ask YouTube to take an adaptation down. Keep monetisation off. The afterword follows an essay published under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, which does not cover adaptations; ask its author before making that film public, or leave it Unlisted.
