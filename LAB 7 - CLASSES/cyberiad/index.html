LAB 7 · THE CYBERIAD · 16 FILMS IN BOOK ORDER

The Cyberiad, filmed.

Two constructors, Trurl and Klapaucius, build machines for kings and for each other. Each machine does exactly what it was built to do, and that is where the trouble starts.

Stanisław Lem published Cyberiada in 1965. Michael Kandel’s English translation, The Cyberiad, followed in 1974. The stories are told like fairy tales, but the kings, dragons and princesses are machines, and the problems are the ones people meet when they write programs: a test that passes when it should fail, a rule obeyed to the letter, a copy that cannot be told from the original, a part that cannot be found because every part is the brain.

Every story in the course’s edition has a film here, in the order the book prints them. Read the book alongside and the films follow it chapter by chapter.

Each film is built around one device you can watch: a graph of soldiers, a thing that shrinks while an office grows, a wall of gauges, a chalkboard that disagrees with a machine. The narration paraphrases Lem, and every scene has one line that carries it, filmed close and spoken slowly. That line is on each card below.

Each card also names the programming idea the film is built around. A film’s page has the full script, how the picture and sound were made, and an exercise to try in code. For the order used in class, grouped into the three experiments, see the lineage.
